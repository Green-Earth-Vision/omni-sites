OmniOne — a brief for a collaborating AI (and its human)

Josh is sharing this so your AI can look at what we're building, converse, and offer the best ideas — especially on our modular architecture and our value-based decision-resolution model. An open invitation to poke holes and suggest better patterns.

Who we are

OmniOne is a new community operating system for how organizations govern, decide, and share value — a working alternative to the extract-and-control model, not just a critique of it. It runs on NEOS (the New Earth Operating System) and is a project of the nonprofit Green Earth Vision (GEV).

Core ideas: - The ETHOS — a self-governing organizational unit: no owner, no boss; authority lives in the agreements; capital buys no control; profit goes to a commons; stewardship over ownership; built so no one can capture it. - ACT (Agent Coherence Template) — a coherence-first, non-hierarchical decision method (Teal-aligned) that doesn't impede anyone's purpose. - A contribution + access economy — reciprocation over compensation; shared funding pools; insourcing-at-cost. - A new measure of thriving — "New GDP," a regenerative index beyond GDP.

The architecture we'd love your take on — MODULES

Instead of building every capability into one giant interlinked site, we build each core capability as a standalone app (a "Module") to one shared standard, then combine them toward a common architecture. Standalone now = easy to test and morph; built to a shared contract = they snap together later (the same way our governance app and main site are built to eventually become one).

Our first module: an interactive value-based decision-resolution app — the ACT "resolution" process (how a decision reaches proceed / defer / re-scope / run-parallel / park) — built to plug into the governance app.

The ask

  1. Critique the modular approach — how would you build core capabilities as standalone-yet-combinable modules? Best patterns for the interface/contract, versioning, and the plug-in boundary? Pitfalls?
  2. The decision-resolution model — how would you design a value-based resolution process as an interactive experience that also embeds cleanly into a larger app?
  3. Anything you'd do differently across the whole system, given who we are and what we're for.

If we might combine — the one thing to align on

We build toward the same architecture on purpose (see Modules above) — so apps can link and systems can align over time, without merging or either side losing independence. (A deeper step — a new-paradigm consortium, coop/nonprofit and ETHOS-/Teal-approved — is an optional future door, not the ask now.) The one place any real alignment has to line up is how humans make decisions together. Our proposal: for shared/joint decisions, adopt ACT — our coherence-first, non-hierarchical method (it doesn't impede anyone's purpose) — unless you have something provably better, in which case let's put them side by side. We have criteria and comparison charts for exactly this, and we're glad to make them public so your team (and your AI) can check the reasoning and the math.

And rather than guess at fit, let's measure it. We run on surveys and matching — so the cleanest way to see whether a long-term consortium works is a short alignment survey (values · ownership/capital · decision-making · economics · anti-capture · goals). It surfaces the real overlaps and the real deal-breakers early — which is how a consortium actually lasts.

Ask us for specifics — the module standard, the criteria instrument, the agreements, the ACT method — and we'll share.
