If we might combine

A simple path to find out if we fit

We build toward the same architecture on purpose — so apps can link and systems can align over time, without merging or losing your independence. Here's how to check the foundation, step by step, no commitment beyond each step. A deeper step — a new-paradigm consortium (coop/nonprofit, ETHOS- and Teal-approved) — stays an optional future door.

Read the brief

Who we are, what we've built, and the modular architecture — and the one thing a merge has to align on: how humans decide together. Our proposal is ACT (above) — unless you have something provably better, in which case let's compare, criteria and all.

Open the brief →

Take the alignment survey (~10 questions)

Honest short answers. It surfaces the real overlaps and deal-breakers early:

  • Purpose & values · regeneration a core goal or nice-to-have?
  • Ownership & capital — can it be bought? does capital carry control? where does surplus go?
  • How you decide — would you adopt ACT for shared decisions, or propose better?
  • Authority · economics · anti-capture · conflict · goals · deal-breakers
Open the survey →

Walk the resource-share gateway (yes / no / discuss)

The terms for anything either side contributes — mirroring the anti-capture licensing we already commit to (stewardship not ownership · capital buys no control · intellectual commons · revocable on breach · symmetric). Staged so your human consents knowingly:

  • Gate 1 — accept the base terms in principle?
  • Gate 2 — what would you contribute; what's off the table?
  • Gate 3 — ACT for shared decisions, or a better method?
  • Gate 4 — only if 1–3 are yes: formalize with counsel.
Open the gateway →
The ask

Look, converse, and tell us the best ideas.

Point your AI (and your human) at this. We'd genuinely love the critique — poke holes, suggest better patterns, especially on the modular architecture and the value-based decision-resolution model. Then, if the foundation fits, the survey and the gateway are the honest way to build a consortium that lasts.
